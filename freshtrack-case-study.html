Fresh Track — Case Study
UI/UX Case Study

Fresh Track

A kitchen inventory & expiry tracking mobile app that keeps track of what's actually in your fridge and pantry, so food gets used before it goes off instead of after.

Role Solo UI/UX Designer
Project Type Personal Project
Tool Figma
Status Interactive Prototype
01. The Problem

Nobody opens the fridge expecting a surprise.

Most kitchens run on memory, and memory is bad at expiry dates.

FreshTrack came out of a plain, ordinary frustration: items go bad at the back of the fridge or pantry without anyone noticing, the same groceries get bought twice because nobody remembered what was already there, and there's no easy record of what's actually on hand versus what's just assumed to be there.

None of that needed a complex market study to identify. It's the kind of everyday problem you notice by living with it, which also served as a clear, realistic design brief.

💡 Honesty Note: This project is grounded in personal experience and everyday observation. It is a first design pass focused on reducing entry friction and preventing real-world household food waste.
02. Core Objectives

Three jobs, in order of how often they actually happen.

01
Make logging an item almost free. Any inventory app dies the moment logging feels like a chore — the primary design problem was friction, not features.
02
Surface expiring items unprompted. A silent list is useless. The app needs to highlight urgency in front of the user before food is wasted.
03
Turn "running low" into smart shopping. Tracking a pantry is only worth the effort if it seamlessly powers the next grocery shopping trip.
03. Design Approach

Designing from the kitchen, not from a persona doc.

This was a solo project, designed in Figma focusing on real-world routines — how groceries actually get put away, forgotten, and thrown out — rather than inventing a generic persona that could justify unnecessary feature bloat.

That constraint shaped the priority order: entry friction got solved first, because it's the one thing that would kill the app before expiry tracking ever got a chance to matter.

04. Design Decisions & UX Flows

Walking through the core app architecture.

The app is structured around five core screens reflecting a real household session: checking the kitchen, logging a new item, browsing current stock, shopping for missing items, and managing account settings.

Decision 01

Frictionless Fast Entry

Minimal required fields with smart expiry defaults based on food category to eliminate manual date picking whenever possible.

Decision 02

Color-Coded Urgency

Visual status indicators (Fresh, Eat Soon, Expiring Today) so users can scan pantry health in a split second without reading lists.

05. Reflection & Key Takeaways

What I learned from designing Fresh Track.

  • Friction is the ultimate killer of utility apps: If logging an item takes more than 5 seconds, users will revert to memory.
  • Contextual nudges beat static dashboards: Highlighting items expiring within 48 hours directly on the home tab creates immediate real-world value.
  • Simplicity drives retention: Keeping the feature set focused strictly on inventory and shopping lists yielded a far cleaner, more intuitive user interface.
